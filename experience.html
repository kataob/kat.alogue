Doctoral Researcher
2023 – 2026
DTU Wind & Energy Systems, Technical University of Denmark · Copenhagen, Denmark

The questions I explored can be boiled down to:

  • What is so special about the design of hybrid renewable power plants (wind + solar PV)?
  • How can we design them to be more (cost) efficient and sustainable?
  • Can we better use the existing in-plant infrastructure of existing wind farms? How much?
  • How to (mathematically) model some engineering practices so optimized solutions are as close to something realistic?
  • What mathematical "black magic" to use to get improved designs of large renewable hybrid plants — fast, efficient, yet accurate?

All are addressed in 5 publications, 1 patent application, and put into the PhD dissertation of 150 pages.

TLDR Hybrid plants are (usually) worth the extra design complexity, and we can (definitely) design them better than we do today — with less uncertainties, and larger positive impacts on people, planet, and profit.

Besides, I got to collaborate with the great TotalEnergies power systems team, live as Emily in Paris for 3 months, communicate my research a gazillion times to industry and academic audiences (and let me tell you, figuring out the academic jargon from a different field is rougher than Reviewer #2), and meet A BUNCH of phenomenal people.
